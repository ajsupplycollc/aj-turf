# How long does artificial turf last in Florida?

**By Jereme Strange, AJ Turf** — years on the manufacturing side of the turf industry, and years installing and repairing turf in Florida.

**The answer:** In Florida, plan on 10–15 years of honest service life from a quality artificial turf install — not the "decades" in some vendor marketing. The manufacturer's warranty runs 8, 16, or 25 years depending on the product you choose, but it covers UV stability and strength only, prorated, and it's separate from realistic everyday life.

## Two different numbers that get sold as one

This is the single most confused point in turf buying, so here it is plainly. **Service life** is how long the yard actually looks and performs like a lawn. **Warranty term** is how long the manufacturer stands behind two specific properties of the product — UV stability and strength — under a prorated schedule. They are not the same number, and a 25-year warranty tier does not mean a 25-year-looking lawn.

When vendor marketing says turf lasts for decades, treat that as a vendor claim about the warranty paperwork, not a promise about your yard. Our honest planning frame — the one we have used consistently for years — is 10–15 years of service life from a quality product, installed on a proper base, and maintained the way the care guide requires.

Just as important is the exclusion list, because it decides real claims: damage from burns, cuts, accidents, and vandalism; wear and abrasion from an inadequate sub-base; faulty installation, including seam failure; improper cleaning or chemicals; failure to maintain the turf per the care guide; acts of God; and melt damage from reflected window heat. Read that middle section again — the base and the installation are warranty issues, not just quality issues.

## What actually decides your years in Florida

**The product.** Denser turf — higher face weight, stronger yarn — resists matting and wear longer in traffic lanes. Carbon-chain quality in the polyethylene yarn drives UV resistance and resilience: longer-chain yarn is stronger and more UV-resistant. A sparse, budget product in a high-traffic Florida yard can mat within a few seasons; that is product life ending early, not a defect.

**The sun.** Florida UV is the aging engine. Lab weathering tests on quality products show strong color and texture retention, but real yards also face a Florida-specific killer: low-E window reflections can concentrate heat past 200°F and melt a stripe of turf in a week, in mild weather. Melted fibers cannot be restored — that section gets cut out and replaced, and the reflection has to be fixed first.

**The base and the install.** Wrinkles, open seams, dips, and washouts are the failures that end installs early, and nearly all of them trace to the base or the perimeter — poor compaction washing out into divots, or spike-secured edges creeping loose under daily heat expansion. An install built on a properly compacted base with a glued, continuous perimeter removes the moving parts that shorten life.

**The maintenance.** The manufacturer's own care guide is a warranty condition: failure to maintain per the guidelines can void coverage. The routine is light but real — brush against the grain to keep fibers standing (weekly in high-traffic areas, monthly in low), rinse periodically, check infill levels and top up, and keep chlorine bleach, caustic or highly acidic cleaners, and metal-bristle brushes away from it. Infill is structural: at our baseline of 1.5–2 lb per square foot, it is part of what holds the fibers upright.

## Florida note

Florida compresses turf aging. Surface temperatures in full summer sun run far above air temperature — university extension measurements in Florida have recorded artificial turf around 174°F on the hottest days — and the daily heat cycle works seams and edges every single day of the year, not just in summer. Add year-round UV, heavy summer rain testing the base's drainage, and humidity feeding algae in shaded, damp spots, and a Florida yard simply lives a harder life than the same product in a mild climate. That is why the honest Florida number is 10–15 years, and why install quality matters more here than anywhere.

> **The honest downside**
> Even a great install is not a forever lawn, and it does not age gracefully forever — traffic lanes mat, color softens, and by the back half of its life the yard will look like a well-kept older lawn, not a new one. The warranty will not rescue appearance: it covers UV stability and strength, prorated, and normal wear and tear is expressly not a defect. It also does not transfer when you sell the house, and our labor warranty is one year — after that, repair labor is yours. Anyone who sells you turf as a 25-year, set-it-and-forget-it surface is selling you the warranty tier, not the yard.

> **The gotcha**
> The bait is a single sentence: "This turf lasts 25 years." What the seller means is that the product *carries a 25-year warranty tier* — prorated, covering two properties, with exclusions that include the bad installation they may be about to give you. Here is the trap inside the trap, and it is the one we have warned homeowners about for years: faulty installation and an inadequate sub-base are warranty exclusions. The cheapest install can quietly cost you the very warranty that was used to sell you the job. Ask to see the actual warranty document for the exact product, before you sign — a real installer will hand it over.

## FAQ

**Does a 25-year warranty mean the turf will look good for 25 years?**
No. The 25-year figure is a warranty tier covering UV stability and strength, on a prorated schedule — it is not a service-life promise. Our honest planning frame for a Florida yard is 10–15 years of service life. Vendor "decades" language describes the paperwork, not the lawn.

**What voids or blocks a turf warranty claim?**
The big ones for homeowners: faulty installation, wear from an inadequate sub-base, failure to maintain per the manufacturer's care guide, damage from chemicals or improper cleaning, and melt from reflected window heat. That is why the install underneath the turf matters as much as the product on top.

**If I sell my house, does the turf warranty go to the buyer?**
No. The manufacturer's warranty is non-transferable — it stays with you as the original purchaser and does not restart or extend with repairs or replacements either. We tell you that before you sign, not after you list the house.

**What maintenance actually protects the lifespan?**
Brushing against the grain to keep fibers standing, periodic rinsing, checking and topping up infill, and prompt attention to small problems before they grow. Never use chlorine bleach, caustic cleaners above pH 9, highly acidic cleaners below pH 5, or metal-bristle brushes — and keep the care guide, because following it is a condition of the manufacturer's coverage.
