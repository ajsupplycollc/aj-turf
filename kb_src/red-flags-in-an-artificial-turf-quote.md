# What are the red flags in a turf quote?

**By Jereme Strange, AJ Turf** — years on the manufacturing side of the turf industry, and years installing and repairing turf in Florida.

**The answer:** The red flags are a price given without seeing the yard, no exact product or face weight named, vague answers on base and edges, most of the money demanded up front, and no written labor warranty. Any one means the quote is hiding its cuts; two or more means walk away and get a measured quote.

## The short version

| Red flag | What it sounds like | What it's usually hiding |
|---|---|---|
| **Price without seeing the yard** | A firm installed price from a phone call, a photo, or your own measurements | A number that creeps between quote and invoice — or a scope that shrinks to fit it |
| **No product named** | "Premium pet turf" with no exact product, no sample, no spec sheet | Freedom to install whatever is on the truck — including a lighter face weight than you paid for |
| **Only a total weight given** | "It's an 80-ounce turf" | The face weight — a heavy backing can make sparse turf weigh like dense turf |
| **Vague base answer** | "We put down a base" — no material, depth, or compaction | A thin or sand-only base, the shortcut behind lumps, washouts, and puddling |
| **Vague edge answer** | "We secure the edges" — method unnamed | Spikes through the turf: point loads that work loose until edges lift |
| **Most of the money up front** | A large deposit before any work, with no staged schedule in writing | Your leverage, gone before the first seam is glued |
| **No written labor warranty** | "We stand behind our work" | Nothing enforceable in year two — and faulty installation is a manufacturer-warranty exclusion |
| **"Pet turf" that's just silica** | A pet quote whose only infill line is plain sand | The odor layer was never in the quote; the yard smells by month three |

## Flag 1: a price that never visited your yard

A turf price is a measurement wearing a number. Slope, access, drainage, existing surface, setbacks, seams against a 15-foot roll width — none of it is visible from a satellite photo, and all of it moves the price. So a firm installed price quoted without an in-person measurement was built to do one of two things: creep upward once the crew discovers your actual yard (price creep between quote and invoice is one of the most common complaint themes in this market), or hold firm by quietly shrinking the scope until the yard fits the number.

The clean model is the opposite: a free onsite measurement, then **one exact written price**. At AJ Turf, most installs run **$9 to $15 a square foot** depending on the turf and the site — a quote dramatically below that band, written sight unseen, is telling you which parts of the build it plans to skip.

## Flag 2: no product named — and the weight trick inside it

A quote should name the exact product, show you a sample, and carry the spec to verify: **face weight and total weight, both**. Face weight is the yarn above the backing — the honest density comparator. Total weight adds backing and coatings and is always bigger, which is why the bait version of this quote lists total weight only: a heavy backing lets a sparse turf "weigh" like a dense one.

The deeper version of this flag is the density bait-and-switch: sold a premium face weight, installed a lighter one — a swap you cannot see with your eyes on install day, on a lawn whose only tell is black backing showing between the stitch rows. The defense is paper plus presence: both numbers in writing before you sign, and the product on the truck checked against the quote when it arrives.

## Flag 3: vague answers on base and edges

These two questions expose an install faster than any other: **"How do you build the base?"** and **"How do you secure the edges?"** Vague answers here aren't shyness — the build *is* the answer, and a real method can be said out loud in a minute. Here is one, as a benchmark: dig about 2–3 inches below the sod line; a screening-sand base at about 1 ton per 100 sq ft finished with a minimum 2-inch compacted layer, compacted to 95% Standard Proctor (ASTM D698) per Synthetic Turf Council guidance; a woven weed barrier on top of the base; and a perimeter of 3×3×3-inch poured concrete with the turf **glued** to it — no spikes through the turf, ever.

Now hear the shortcut catalog against it: a **sand-only base** with no compaction spec (shifts, washes, telegraphs lumps within a season or two); **spikes through the turf** as the whole perimeter plan (each spike a point load that works loose under heat cycling until edges lift and panels crawl); **seams strip-glued or glued to wet backing** with no cure weight (the seam opens within the first year); **plain silica sold as a pet system** (urine held against the backing, odor by month three). Every one of those is a year-one or year-two repair call wearing a cheaper quote as a disguise — seams opening, wrinkles and edge lift after the first summer, and pet odor are the top complaint themes in this business, and each traces to a line the quote left vague.

There's a second cost to the vague base, and it's legal rather than cosmetic: **an inadequate sub-base and faulty installation are exclusions in the manufacturer's warranty.** The cheap base doesn't just risk the yard — it can cost you the claim on the turf itself.

## Flag 4: most of the money up front

Staging tells you who carries the risk. A written schedule that pays as work happens keeps both sides honest; a demand for most of the contract price before a shovel touches the ground moves all of it onto you. Compare against a transparent shape: AJ Turf stages **30% to schedule / 35% the first day on site / 35% on completion**, with deposits always non-refundable and stated that way in writing. Non-refundable is not itself a flag — unstated terms, and a front-loaded demand with no staging behind it, are. Whatever the shape, get it in the contract with the agreed install window: AJ Turf installs are typically scheduled **within two weeks**, and a paid job left sitting unstarted and uncommunicated is the pattern behind the worst stories in this industry.

## Flag 5: no written labor warranty

"We stand behind our work" is a sentence, not a warranty. The written version names two separate things: the **manufacturer's warranty on the product** — at AJ Turf, 8, 16, or 25 years depending on the product, covering UV stability and strength only, prorated, non-transferable — and the **installer's labor warranty** — ours is **1 year**, in writing. Ask to see the actual manufacturer document for your product before you sign, not a summary of it. And treat one phrase as an automatic flag: any quote implying the *manufacturer* covers labor or reimburses install costs as a standard term. In the AJ Turf warranty story there is no manufacturer labor coverage to promise — a quote that leans on one is leaning on a term that doesn't exist in writing anywhere.

## The quiet flags

- **No questions about your dogs, drainage, or windows.** A quote taken without asking where the dogs go, where water pools, or which low-E windows face the yard is a quote that hasn't looked for the three problems that generate repair calls: odor, puddling, and reflection melt.
- **Ghosting after the deposit.** Across this market, the dominant complaints are process failures — no-shows, slow follow-up, deposits taken with work stalled — not product failures. Responsiveness before the sale is the sample of responsiveness after it.
- **No HOA line.** If your yard needs HOA approval, a quote that hands you a packet and wishes you luck has left weeks of work with you. AJ Turf prepares **and submits** the application on the homeowner's behalf — ask whether the other quote does.
- **Too small to be real, too big to check.** AJ Turf installs start at 1,000 sq ft; repairs are assessed free onsite with no minimum. A company with no stated minimums, no stated assessment, and no stated timeline has left itself every exit.

## Florida note

Southwest Florida sharpens every flag. Flat Cape Coral and Fort Myers lots make the base and drainage answers decisive — a yard that still puddles 30 minutes after rain was diagnosed at the quote, whether anyone said so or not. Saturated summer sand is exactly the ground spikes let go in. Florida's Rule 62-308.100 now makes infill material and a washed, pervious subgrade legal requirements, not preferences — a Florida quote that has never heard of the rule is behind the law as well as the craft. And year-round heat runs the expansion-contraction cycle that turns a vague edge answer into noon wrinkles by the first summer.

> **The honest downside**
> A red flag is evidence, not a verdict — a small, honest installer can be sloppy with paperwork and solid with a plate compactor, and a polished quote can be printed by a company that ghosts in month two. Checking flags also costs you something real: time, a second and third onsite visit, and sometimes a higher price, because the method that answers every question on this page — concrete border, named infill, written warranties — genuinely costs more to build. What the flags buy you is not certainty. It's the ability to see *where* a cheap quote is cheap before the cheap parts are buried under your lawn.

> **The gotcha**
> The deepest gotcha in a turf quote is that **the failures are scheduled, not accidental.** Every shortcut on this page has a known arrival date — seams in year one, edge lift after the first summer, odor by month three — safely after final payment. The quote isn't lying about install day; the lawn will look great on install day. It's betting you won't connect the year-two symptoms back to the lines it left blank. Fill the blanks in before you sign, while the leverage is still yours.

## FAQ

**Is a non-refundable deposit a red flag?**
Not by itself — AJ Turf's deposits are always non-refundable, and we say so in writing. The flag is the shape and the silence: most of the money demanded up front, no staged schedule, no agreed install window in the contract. Stated terms you can read are the opposite of a flag.

**A quote is much cheaper than everyone else's. Is that automatically a flag?**
Not automatically — but it has to survive the questions. Same exact product with both weight numbers, a real base with a compaction spec, a continuous glued edge instead of spikes, named infill at named rates, written warranty terms. Cheap quotes usually fail on the rows, not the price; read our spec-for-spec comparison page and line them up.

**What's the single best question to ask a turf installer?**
Two, and they're a pair: "How do you build the base?" and "How do you secure the edges?" A real method answers both in a minute, in specifics — material, depth, compaction, border, glue. Vagueness on either is the highest-information answer a quote can give you.

**The quote says the turf is under warranty for 25 years. What should I check?**
Which warranty, covering what, held by whom. The manufacturer tiers (8, 16, or 25 years by product) cover UV stability and strength only, are prorated and non-transferable, and exclude faulty installation. The installer's own labor warranty is a separate line — AJ Turf's is 1 year. Ask to see the actual document for your product before you sign.
