# How are artificial turf seams joined, and why do they fail?

**By Jereme Strange, AJ Turf** — years on the manufacturing side of the turf industry, and years installing and repairing turf in Florida.

**The answer:** Turf comes in rolls 15 feet wide, so most yards have seams. A good seam butts two pieces with their blades leaning the same way, sits on seam tape pinned down underneath, gets glue in an "S" pattern on both sides of the tape, and is weighted while the glue sets. Seams fail when the glue goes onto wet backing, gets no pressure while it cures, or is spread too thin, and when pieces are laid facing different directions.

## The short version

| Step | What it does | What goes wrong when it's skipped |
|---|---|---|
| Plan the cuts | Seams land where they're least visible | Seams across the main walking path or the view from the house |
| Match direction | Every piece leans the same way | Two pieces read as two different lawns, forever |
| Pin the tape | Seam tape is fastened to the base under the turf | Tape shifts while the glue is wet |
| Glue in an "S" pattern | Both edges bond to the tape | Thin glue lines that let go in the first summer |
| Weight it | Holds the edges down while the glue sets | Edges curl up before the bond forms |

## How is a seam built?

The two pieces are trimmed so the stitch rows line up and the edges meet without a gap or an overlap. Seam tape is centered under the joint and pinned to the base, which is one of the two places nails belong in a turf install (the other is the weed barrier). Turf glue goes onto the tape in an "S" pattern on each side of the center line, the edges are laid into it, and the seam is weighted, typically with bags of infill, so the backing stays pressed into the glue while it sets.

Glue goes on sparingly. Too much seeps up through the drainage holes and mats the blades together into a stiff line you can see and feel. How much glue a job takes depends on its seams and perimeter, which is why nobody can honestly quote you a per-square-foot glue number.

## Why does direction matter so much?

Turf is directional: the blades lean one way, and the same turf looks lighter or darker depending on which way you're looking at it. Lay two pieces facing different directions and the seam becomes a color change that no brushing fixes. It's one of the most common things we see on do-it-yourself jobs. Our rule: in a back yard the blades lean toward the house, so you look into the best face walking out the back door; in a front yard they lean toward the street.

## Why do seams fail?

- **Glue on wet backing.** Adhesive won't bond to a damp backing. A seam glued after a rain shower or on dew-wet turf can look fine for weeks, then open.
- **No pressure while it cures.** A seam that isn't weighted lets the edges lift off the tape before the glue sets.
- **Starved adhesive.** A thin, skipped or rushed glue line saves a few dollars and gives up the bond.
- **Heat movement.** Turf expands and contracts every day. A weak seam is where that movement shows up first.

## Florida note

Southwest Florida runs the heat cycle hard, and the rainy season brings afternoon storms that can wet a job mid-install. Both are reasons seams get glued dry and given their full set time, even when the day's schedule is tight.

> **The honest downside**
> Seams are never truly invisible in every light. A well-built seam is very hard to spot from standing height, but low-angle sun can still catch a line. Good planning puts seams where they matter least. And a seam that failed because of a bad base (dips pulling the joint apart) won't stay fixed until the base is fixed too.

> **The gotcha**
> Ask any installer how they keep a seam pressed down while the glue sets, and whether they'll glue on a wet day. If the answer to the first is a shrug and the second is "it's fine," you're looking at the seam you'll be calling about next summer.

## FAQ

**Can an open seam be fixed without replacing the turf?**
Usually, yes. If the turf pieces and the base underneath are sound, the seam is opened up, cleaned, re-taped and re-glued. If the base has sunk and is pulling the joint apart, the base gets fixed first.

**Why does my lawn look like two different colors on either side of a seam?**
The pieces were laid with the blades leaning in different directions. Brushing won't fix it, because the color difference comes from the direction of the fibers. The fix is lifting and re-laying one side.

**Should a seam ever be nailed?**
The tape underneath is pinned, but nails through the turf at a seam are a shortcut. A properly glued seam doesn't need them, and nails through the turf work loose over time.
