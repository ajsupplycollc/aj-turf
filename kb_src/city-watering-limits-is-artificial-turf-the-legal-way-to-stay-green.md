# My city limits watering — is turf the legal way to keep a green yard?

**By Jereme Strange, AJ Turf** — years on the manufacturing side of the turf industry, and years installing and repairing turf in Florida.

**The answer:** In the strictest zones, yes — effectively. Where an irrigation ban makes a lawn impossible to water legally, turf stays green because it is not irrigated at all. Unincorporated Lee County limits watering to one day a week in dry season, and a South Florida Water Management District order has banned irrigation-system watering for well users in northeast Cape Coral.

## Why this is a legal question at all

Watering restrictions regulate *irrigation* — when and how you may water a living lawn. Artificial turf doesn't enter that system, because a turf yard is never watered to keep it alive or green. It's green in February for the same reason it's green in August: it isn't grass.

That distinction is written into Florida's turf framework from the other direction, too. The statewide synthetic turf standard (Rule 62-308.100) states that in-ground irrigation cannot be used to irrigate synthetic turf areas. In other words, the compliant configuration for a turf yard is an unwatered one — which is why AJ Turf caps the sprinkler heads inside the turf footprint and leaves them in place, while heads in planting beds outside the turf stay live and stay under the normal watering rules.

So the honest framing is not that turf is a loophole. It's that the restrictions govern a thing turf doesn't do. In most of Lee County, that makes turf a convenience — a green yard without scheduling your lawn around watering days. In the Phase IV zone, it makes turf something stronger: where irrigation systems are banned outright for well users, a natural lawn cannot legally be kept alive on that water source, and turf is the only green option left standing.

## What the restrictions actually look like here

Unincorporated Lee County runs a year-round conservation ordinance. From February 1 through May 31 — the dry season, exactly when lawns brown out — watering drops to **one day a week**: odd-numbered addresses on Saturday, even-numbered on Sunday, with no watering between 9 a.m. and 5 p.m. The rest of the year allows two days a week. The county has warned that the 2025 rainy season underdelivered and aquifers entered dry season stressed, which is the direction these rules travel: tighter, not looser.

Cape Coral's standard schedule is two days a week citywide. Layered on top of that, the South Florida Water Management District's Modified Phase IV Water Shortage Order — effective May 2, 2025 — covers northeast Cape Coral and parts of unincorporated Lee County served by Mid-Hawthorn Aquifer wells. For those well users, irrigation systems were banned outright: hand-watering with a self-canceling nozzle only, on Wednesdays, Saturdays, and Sundays. In May 2026 the order was eased to Modified Phase II — once-weekly, address-based — for part of the zone. Part of the zone. The rest still lives under the stricter order, and the direction of travel in this aquifer is restriction, not relief.

Check which schedule actually applies to your address before you plan around any of this — city, county, and water-management-district rules stack, and the strictest one that covers your lot is the one that governs your lawn.

## What turf still uses water for — said honestly

Turf is not a zero-water surface in every sense, and this page won't pretend otherwise. Owners rinse turf: rain is the best cleanser, and periodic rinsing in dry spells is part of normal care — rinsing dust and debris, rinsing a dog's area, rinsing a hot surface to cool it. That is occasional hose use for cleaning, not an irrigation schedule keeping something alive, and it's the difference between a yard that needs water to exist and a yard that occasionally gets hosed down. Planting beds, trees, and anything else alive in the yard still need water under the normal rules — turf ends the lawn's water demand, not the landscape's.

## FAQ

**Is it legal to have a green turf yard during a watering ban?**
Yes. Watering restrictions govern irrigation — watering a living lawn. Artificial turf isn't irrigated; Florida's rule actually prohibits using in-ground irrigation on turf areas. A turf yard stays green during a ban because there's no watering to restrict.

**I'm in the Cape Coral Phase IV zone on a well. What are my options for a green yard?**
Under the order, irrigation systems were banned outright for well users in the zone — hand-watering with a self-canceling nozzle only, Wed/Sat/Sun — and part of the zone has since eased to once-weekly under Phase II. A natural lawn is extremely difficult to keep alive under either. Turf is the option that doesn't depend on the order changing.

**Do I have to remove my sprinkler system to install turf?**
No. On an AJ Turf install the heads inside the turf area are capped and left in place, and heads in planting beds stay live. Florida's rule permits requiring removal, but it doesn't mandate it — capping is the compliant, reversible configuration.

**Does turf need any water at all?**
Occasionally, for care — rinsing dust and debris in dry spells, rinsing pet areas, or wetting the surface to cool it. That's hose use for cleaning, not an irrigation schedule, and it's a fraction of what keeping a lawn alive on one watering day a week demands.

**What if watering restrictions are lifted next year?**
Then a natural lawn gets easier again — and your turf is unchanged: still no mowing, no lawn watering schedule, no dry-season browning. Buy turf because you want the yard it gives you; treat the restriction relief question as a reason to be sure, not a reason to rush.
