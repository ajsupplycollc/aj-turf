# When is the best time of year to install turf in Southwest Florida?

**By Jereme Strange, AJ Turf** — years on the manufacturing side of the turf industry, and years installing and repairing turf in Florida.

**The answer:** For most Southwest Florida homeowners, fall into early winter is the sweet spot: seasonal owners are back, projects get planned October through December, and the new yard is ready before dry-season watering restrictions bite from February through May. Turf can be installed in other months too — the real scheduling enemy is storm season, which pauses work around hurricanes.

## The short version

| Window | What drives it |
|---|---|
| Oct–Dec | Seasonal owners return and plan projects; yard is finished before dry season |
| Feb–May | Dry-season watering restrictions (one day a week in unincorporated Lee County) convert fence-sitters as natural lawns brown out |
| Jun 1–Nov 30 | Hurricane season — installs pause around storms; post-storm damage drives fall repair inquiries |
| Late summer / early fall | Commercial deadline: patios and event venues want turf done before season starts |

## Why fall into early winter wins

Southwest Florida's seasonal population peaks roughly November through April — snowbirds, dry season, and the best outdoor weather of the year arriving together. Seasonal owners are prime turf buyers for a specific reason: they want a yard that's perfect on arrival, needs nothing while they're gone June through October, and survives the summer without a lawn service babysitting it.

That makes the decision window October through December. Returning owners walk their yards, see what the summer did to the grass, and plan projects while they're here to meet installers, approve the work in person, and enjoy the finished yard all season. An install finished in the fall is also finished *before* the problem months: when dry-season watering restrictions arrive in February, the turf yard is already done and the natural lawn's worst stretch is simply not your problem anymore.

There's a practical scheduling point, too: AJ Turf installs are typically scheduled within about two weeks, at about two days per 1,000 square feet. A project decided in November is a finished yard well before the holidays are over — not a spring promise.

## The dry-season window: February through May

If fall is when people plan, late winter and spring are when the fence-sitters convert. Water restrictions bite hardest February through May — one watering day a week in unincorporated Lee County — exactly when natural lawns brown out in public. The yard that embarrassed you all winter becomes the project you finally schedule.

Installing in this window is really about what the *next* dry season looks like: the turf goes in while the problem is visible and undeniable, and it's established long before the summer rains test its drainage. There's no agronomic deadline the way there is with seed or sod — turf doesn't need a growing season — so a spring install is driven by motivation and schedule, not by the product's calendar.

## Storm season: workable, with an asterisk

Hurricane season runs June 1 through November 30, and it cuts both ways. Installs pause around storms — you don't excavate a yard with a hurricane in the Gulf, and you don't glue seams onto a wet backing in summer downpours either, because glue applied to wet backing never forms its bond. Summer scheduling is a matter of working around the weather rather than through it.

The same season creates the fall's demand. Post-storm "my yard is destroyed" moments drive repair and replacement inquiries once the weather clears — and the turf house on the street that still looks perfect after a storm is its own advertisement. If your yard was damaged, repairs start with a free onsite assessment, and deciding in the fall puts the work in the year's best planning window anyway.

One group plans *against* this season deliberately: seasonal-rush businesses — restaurants with patios, event venues — want turf done before their season, which means late-summer and early-fall installs, finished before the November crowds arrive.

## When not to overthink it

Turf has no planting season. It doesn't germinate, root, or need to establish before a frost that Southwest Florida barely gets. The product can go in any month the weather allows the work; the calendar questions that actually matter are yours — when you'll be here to enjoy it, when your lawn's failures are costing you most, and whether a storm is on the calendar. The seasonal logic above is about timing the decision, not about a window in which turf "takes" better.

## Florida note

Everything on this page is downstream of one fact: Southwest Florida's year is split between a dry season with watering restrictions (worst February–May) and a wet, storm-season half (June–November) — with the population itself swelling November through April. The install calendar that fits this place is: plan when you return in the fall, be finished before the dry season tests every natural lawn on the street, and let storm season be a scheduling factor rather than a surprise. Cape Coral owners on wells in the water-shortage zone have an extra push: in the strictest phase, a living lawn can't legally be kept watered at all, and that order doesn't follow the tourist calendar.

> **The honest downside**
> The best window is also the busiest one. October through December is when every returning owner in Southwest Florida has the same idea, and while AJ Turf typically schedules within about two weeks, deciding in the fall rush means sharing the calendar with the rush. And "best time" logic can become an excuse to wait a full year for a marginally better month; turf installed in an off-peak month serves you identically for its whole life.

## FAQ

**Can artificial turf be installed in the summer?**
Yes, with weather flexibility. Hurricane season (June 1–November 30) pauses installs around storms, and summer rain matters to the work itself — seam glue on a wet backing doesn't bond. Summer jobs get scheduled around the weather, not forced through it.

**When should a snowbird schedule an install?**
In the October–December return window. That's when you're here for the onsite estimate, the install, and the walkthrough — and the finished yard is in place before dry-season restrictions arrive in February and before you leave again in the spring.

**Does turf need to "settle in" before hurricane season?**
There's no curing season for turf the way there's an establishment period for sod. What a pre-storm-season install gives you is a finished yard — glued perimeter, seams cured, infill in place — rather than a half-built one when weather arrives. The scheduling reason to avoid storm week is the work itself, not the product.

**Is there a cheaper season to install?**
Not at AJ Turf. Pricing is $9–$15 per square foot for most installs regardless of the month, with the exact price from the free onsite estimate. Any installer whose price swings hard with the calendar is pricing demand, not your yard.

**Commercial property — when should we install before season?**
Late summer into early fall. Restaurants, patios, and event venues that need turf performing for the November–April rush should have the work finished before the crowds — which means deciding while it's still hot.
