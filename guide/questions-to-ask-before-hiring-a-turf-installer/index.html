Questions to Ask Before You Hire a Turf Installer

The short answer

Ask how they build the base, how they secure the edges, who holds the turf warranty, what their own labor warranty covers, and how payments are staged. Get one written price based on an in-person measurement, with the exact product named.

Homeowner complaints about turf installers tend to fall into the same few patterns: no-shows and slow follow-up, a final bill that doesn't match the quote, deposits taken and work that stalls, and warranty problems after the fact. These questions are designed to catch each one before you sign.

1. "How do you build the base?"

Listen for excavation, a screening sand or aggregate base, and compaction. Manufacturer warranties typically exclude wear caused by an inadequate sub-base, so a weak base can cost you the warranty as well as the lawn.

2. "How do you secure the edges?"

Spikes through the lawn are common and can work loose over time. Ask what holds the perimeter. A glued edge on a poured concrete border is one sturdier option.

3. "Who holds the turf warranty, and can I see it?"

Manufacturer warranties usually cover the turf itself, like UV stability and premature wear, and not the installation. They often exclude faulty installation, and many are prorated and not transferable. Ask for the actual warranty document for the exact product, not a summary.

4. "What does your labor warranty cover, and for how long?"

Ripples, seams opening and edges lifting are installation issues. Get the labor warranty in writing.

5. "Is this price based on an in-person measurement?"

A price from a photo or satellite view is an estimate. Ask whether the written price can change, and why. Turf comes in rolls about 15 feet wide, so a fair quote includes an allowance for cuts around your yard's shape, and it should say so.

6. "Which product exactly, and can I see a sample?"

The quote should name the turf, not just "premium turf." Feel samples in person, especially for pet areas and putting greens.

7. "How are payments staged?"

Payments tied to progress protect both sides, for example a deposit to schedule, a payment when work starts, and the balance after a final walkthrough. Be cautious about paying most of the job before any work begins.

8. "Who is on site, and are they insured?"

Ask who does the work and ask to see proof of insurance for the crew.

9. "Will you help with my HOA?"

Many HOAs require architectural review before turf goes in. A good installer can give you the product specs and install details the HOA asks for.

Red flags

A price given without anyone seeing the yard. No product named on the quote. Most of the money up front. Vague answers about the base or edges. No written labor warranty.
